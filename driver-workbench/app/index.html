Club by club

Each club against windows set for —’s own speed from the TrackMan LPGA Tour averages, using the same sessions as the whole bag above.

How the windows are set
  • Smash: at least the tour’s ball speed ÷ club speed for that club, less 0.04. The driver uses the target smash from the driver dashboard.
  • Launch: within 2° of the tour average. The driver uses the driver dashboard’s window for the logged club speed.
  • Spin: within 15% of the tour average, scaled to this golfer’s club speed with the club (spin rises with speed for the same strike).
  • Descent: no more than 3° flatter than the tour’s landing angle, so the ball stops on greens.
  • Carry vs tour: the tour carry scaled by this golfer’s ball speed ÷ the tour’s. Well short usually means launch or spin is costing carry, or spin reads low (see the banner above).
  • Woods past the 5-wood and hybrids use the 7-wood row; 1–3 irons use the 4 iron row; wedges past the pitching wedge have no tour row. Five shots or more are needed to judge a club. Source: TrackMan LPGA Tour averages.

What’s working and what isn’t

Patterns across —’s rounds, from hole-by-hole scores where they were entered and the 1–5 ratings; range or lesson entries are left out. The date range above applies.

How this is judged
  • Working: fairways hit 60% or more, greens in regulation 50% or more, up and down 40% or more, a three-putt a round or fewer, the best-scoring hole type, and the highest-rated area when it is 3.5 or more.
  • Needs work: fairways under 45%, greens under 30%, up and down under 20%, two or more three-putts or three or more doubles a round, the costliest hole type, and the lowest-rated area when it is 3 or less.
  • Each needs enough holes to mean something: 10 fairways, 18 greens, 8 missed greens or 9 holes of a type.
  • Range against course: the share of drives in the last 4 weeks on the range that stay inside the fairway width picked at the top, against fairways hit on the course. A gap of 15 points or more is flagged.

Topics to cover

In order of priority. Each status comes from —’s latest week and the targets above.

  1. 1. Numbers you can trust

    Why: every other topic depends on speeds that add up.

    How: import the GSPro CSV instead of typing; at least — driver swings a week; same device and ball each week; note the simulator’s altitude setting, which changes carry but not speeds.

  2. 2. Strike quality (smash)

    Why: each 0.01 of smash is about 1 mph of ball speed, roughly 2 yards.

    How: face spray every session; tee-gate drill for heel and toe misses; swings at 80% effort building up to full; keep tee height constant; re-check smash every time speed jumps.

  3. 3. Speed training

    Why: each mph of club speed is worth about 2.5 to 3 yards, if the strike holds.

    How: stay on the program three times a week; test on the same device each week; expect a plateau after the first phase; count a gain only when smash holds at the new speed.

  4. 4. Attack angle and launch

    Why: hitting up raises launch while cutting spin, the only change that adds both carry and roll.

    How: ball off the lead heel; tee so half the ball sits above the crown; trail shoulder lower at address; keep the head behind the ball through impact.

  5. 5. Spin

    Why: too much spin balloons and stops; too little drops out of the air.

    How: spin follows attack angle and strike height, so fix those first; change loft or weights only once delivery settles.

  6. 6. Start line and curve

    Why: a faster swing that sprays loses more than it gains on the course.

    How: watch the offline spread each week, plus face to path and club path when the monitor reports them.

  7. 7. Driver fit

    Why: loft and weights tune launch and spin around the delivery; fit too early and you fit the old swing.

    How: once launch and strike are on target, test loft one degree up and down and weight back against forward, 5 to 10 balls each; shaft and length after that.

  8. 8. On-course transfer

    Ongoing

    How: track drives in rounds: fairways hit, carry over a known hazard, and total on firm against soft days.

How the numbers are worked out
  • Smash factor is ball speed ÷ club speed. A week is flagged when that differs from the device’s smash by more than —. For imported weeks, the speeds are averages of the driver shots that both speeds were read for.
  • Carry and total come from a ball-flight model (drag, lift and spin decay, then bounce and roll) calibrated to TrackMan tour averages and TrackMan’s optimizer chart. Trust the changes more than the exact yards, which can be off by about 5 yards.
  • Until a week has its own launch and spin, the look uses the most recent week that has them, or the earlier shot in the benchmarks.
  • The attack-angle step picks the best launch and spin reachable with the target attack angle, assuming about — rpm of spin per degree of spin loft for each mph of club speed and at least — rpm.
  • Weeks without a logged temperature use the air temperature set above. Fairway firmness changes roll only.
  • Targets follow each golfer’s own speed. At — mph club speed, with the target smash and attack angle above, the model’s best launch is —° with — rpm, so the windows are launch —–—° and spin —–— rpm. Smash below — is a priority.
  • Imported files keep every club and every shot, so later versions can analyze irons, wedges and woods from the same history.
